Machine Learning for Biology | Day 70
Chapter 7: Immune cell subtype discovery from single-cell RNA-seq

That is the series. Seven chapters, seven models, and the basics of machine learning, one idea at a time.

Chapter 1, logistic regression: a straight line drawn through a decision.
Chapter 2, a decision tree: a model you can read out loud, one split at a time.
Chapter 3, a random forest: many trees voting together.
Chapter 4, gradient boosting: each new tree fixing what the last one got wrong.
Chapter 5, a support vector machine: the widest possible gap between two outcomes.
Chapter 6, a neural network: learning on its own which combinations matter.
Chapter 7, k-means: no answer key at all, only the data.

The first six were supervised. They learned from examples someone had already labelled. The last was unsupervised. It had to find the groups by itself, and it showed exactly where that gets hard. Sorting the cells was the easy part. Deciding how many groups there are was not.

The models changed from chapter to chapter. The approach did not. Fix the rules before running anything. Compare against a simple baseline. Share what didn't work as openly as what did.

That approach is better than the model. A result nobody can check is only an opinion.

The code, the rules and every number from this chapter are in the repository.
